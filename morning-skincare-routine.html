Morning skincare routine for wrinkles — simple steps that work

A morning routine doesn't need to be complex. A focused four-step approach done consistently is more effective than an elaborate routine used inconsistently. Here's what to do and why.

Morning routine at a glance

  • Step 1: Gentle cleanser (or water rinse if skin was cleansed well the night before)
  • Step 2: Vitamin C or antioxidant serum (optional but beneficial)
  • Step 3: Moisturiser suited to your skin type
  • Step 4: SPF 30–50 — non-negotiable, final step

Step 1 — Gentle cleanse

In the morning, your skin has not been exposed to pollution, makeup, or heavy environmental debris — it has primarily been in contact with your pillowcase and your evening skincare products. A gentle cleanse or even a plain water rinse is often sufficient.

If you use a foaming cleanser in the morning, choose one that does not leave skin feeling tight or stripped. Very dry or sensitive skin often benefits from switching to a cream or gel cleanser in the morning, or simply rinsing with cool water.

What to look for: low pH, no sulphates (SLS/SLES) if you have dry or sensitive skin, fragrance-free.

Step 2 — Vitamin C or antioxidant serum (optional)

Morning is the ideal time for antioxidants, particularly vitamin C. Antioxidants help neutralise free radicals generated by UV exposure and pollution during the day — they work alongside your SPF to protect skin from environmental stress.

Apply vitamin C serum to clean, dry skin and allow it to absorb for a minute before the next step. Look for L-ascorbic acid (the most effective form) in opaque or airtight packaging, at 10–20% concentration.

If vitamin C irritates your skin, niacinamide is a gentler antioxidant alternative that also supports skin tone and barrier health.

This step is optional but adds value — particularly for skin that has concerns around pigmentation or uneven tone.

Step 3 — Moisturiser

A good moisturiser maintains skin hydration throughout the day, supports the barrier, and prepares the skin for SPF application. Choose a texture and formulation suited to your skin type:

  • Dry skin: richer cream with ceramides, hyaluronic acid, glycerin
  • Oily or combination: lightweight gel-cream or fluid moisturiser
  • Sensitive: fragrance-free, minimal-ingredient formula with ceramides or peptides
  • Mature skin (50s+): richer cream with ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and peptides

Allow moisturiser to absorb before applying SPF — usually 30–60 seconds is enough.

Step 4 — SPF 30–50 (non-negotiable)

The final and most important step in any morning routine. SPF protects against UV damage — the leading external cause of premature skin ageing, fine lines, pigmentation, and uneven texture.

Apply SPF after moisturiser, as the last product in your routine. Use a generous amount — at least ¼ teaspoon for the face, more if you are also applying to your neck and décolletage (which you should be).

SPF in foundation is not enough

The amount of SPF typically applied via tinted moisturiser or foundation is far below what's needed to achieve the stated SPF level. Use a dedicated SPF product as a separate step, then apply makeup on top.

Makeup compatibility

A morning routine including SPF is fully compatible with makeup. Allow your SPF to settle for 2–3 minutes before applying foundation or tinted products. Look for SPF formulations designed to be worn under makeup — these tend to have a lighter, less tacky finish.

Some women prefer SPF tinted moisturisers or SPF foundations as their final step — these can work, but make sure the SPF level is adequate (30–50) and that you are applying enough product to get the stated protection.

The busy version — three minutes or less

If time is limited, the absolute minimum morning routine for skin health is:

  1. Quick water rinse
  2. Combined SPF moisturiser (one product that delivers both hydration and sun protection)

This takes under two minutes and covers the most important bases. Complexity can be added gradually as time and budget allow.

What not to use in the morning

  • Retinol: a night-only ingredient. It breaks down in sunlight and should not be applied in the morning.
  • AHAs at high concentration: these can increase photosensitivity. If you use them, use them at night and ensure SPF is thorough the next morning.
  • Physical scrubs: unnecessary and potentially irritating in the morning routine.
