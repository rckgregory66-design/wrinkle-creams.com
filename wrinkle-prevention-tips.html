Prevention

Wrinkle prevention tips that actually make a difference

You cannot stop your skin from ageing, and that is not the goal. But there are well-supported habits that can slow the process and help your skin stay healthy and comfortable for longer. Here they are — without guilt or pressure.

A note on tone

This guide is about supporting your skin health if that's what you choose. Ageing is normal and natural. The goal here is not to eliminate signs of age, but to help you make informed choices if you want to support your skin over the long term.

1. Daily SPF — the most important step

This comes first because the evidence for it is strongest. UV radiation is one of the primary external causes of premature skin ageing — contributing to fine lines, pigmentation, loss of elasticity, and uneven texture. This damage accumulates over decades of daily exposure, even on cloudy days and even indoors near windows.

Daily SPF use is widely considered by skin professionals to be the single most evidence-backed thing you can do to slow visible skin ageing. SPF 30 is the minimum; SPF 50 offers more protection. Apply every morning as the last step in your morning routine.

2. Hydration — inside and out

Well-hydrated skin looks better and is more resilient. This includes both internal hydration (drinking enough water) and external hydration (using good moisturisers). Dehydrated skin is more prone to fine lines looking pronounced, and the skin barrier is less effective when moisture levels are low.

Externally: look for hyaluronic acid, glycerin, ceramides, and a moisturiser that suits your skin type.

3. Consistent sleep

During sleep, the skin repairs itself and produces growth factors that support skin renewal. Chronically poor sleep is associated with duller, less resilient skin and a compromised skin barrier. Getting consistent, adequate sleep is one of the least glamorous but most genuinely effective skin health habits.

4. Smoking avoidance

Smoking is strongly linked to premature skin ageing — including increased fine lines, dull skin tone, uneven texture, and impaired collagen production. This guide does not preach, but the evidence linking smoking and accelerated skin ageing is well established.

5. Gentle cleansing

Harsh cleansers that strip the skin barrier can leave skin dry, reactive, and more vulnerable. A gentle, low-foaming cleanser that removes the day without leaving skin feeling tight is ideal for supporting skin health long term. Avoid very hot water, which also strips natural oils.

6. Consistent moisturising

A good moisturiser used consistently every day does more for most people's skin than occasional use of expensive actives. The skin barrier needs regular support to maintain its function. Choose a moisturiser suited to your skin type and use it morning and evening.

7. A consistent routine — more important than complexity

Three steps used consistently every day will outperform a ten-step routine used irregularly. Consistency is the foundation of any effective skincare approach. Establish your core routine (cleanse, moisturise, SPF) and keep it sustainable before adding anything more complex.

8. Avoiding over-exfoliation

It is possible to exfoliate too much. Over-exfoliation — using acids and physical exfoliants too frequently — can damage the skin barrier, cause chronic irritation, and make skin more sensitive and reactive. If your skin feels raw, tight, or stinging regularly, less exfoliation is usually the answer. Once or twice a week with a low-strength acid is plenty for most people.

9. Skin barrier care

The skin barrier is the outermost layer that protects against moisture loss and external stress. When it is well supported — with ceramides, gentle products, and adequate moisture — skin is more resilient, comfortable, and less reactive. When it is compromised — by over-exfoliation, harsh products, or insufficient moisture — skin becomes dry, sensitive, and more prone to visible fine lines.

Prioritising barrier health is especially important in your 50s and 60s, when the barrier naturally becomes less efficient. See our dry skin guide and sensitive skin guide for more detail.

Summary — simple habits that matter

  • Apply SPF 30–50 every morning — rain or shine
  • Drink enough water each day
  • Moisturise morning and evening with a product suited to your skin type
  • Cleanse gently — avoid hot water and harsh foaming cleansers
  • Prioritise consistent sleep
  • Do not over-exfoliate — less is more
  • Support your skin barrier with ceramide-rich products if skin is dry or sensitive
  • Keep your routine consistent — give products 8–12 weeks before judging them
  • Introduce one new product at a time so you can identify what works (or doesn't)

Frequently asked questions

  • Daily SPF 30 or above is the single most effective wrinkle prevention step. UV damage is the leading external cause of premature skin ageing. After SPF, not smoking, staying well hydrated, getting adequate sleep, and maintaining a consistent gentle skincare routine with antioxidants all contribute to reducing preventable skin ageing.

  • Staying well hydrated supports overall skin health, but drinking water does not directly prevent wrinkles. Dehydrated skin can look duller and fine lines may appear more pronounced, but the primary driver of wrinkle formation is UV damage, collagen decline, and facial muscle movement — not hydration levels alone.

  • Yes. Sleeping on your side or stomach can cause sleep lines from repeated compression of the face against the pillow. These lines often fade in the morning when young, but can become more persistent over time. Sleeping on your back, or using a silk pillowcase to reduce friction, can help minimise this.

  • Yes. A diet high in antioxidants (fruits, vegetables, green tea) helps protect against oxidative stress, which contributes to skin ageing. High sugar intake can contribute to glycation — a process that can make skin look less firm and luminous over time. A balanced diet supports skin health alongside good topical skincare.

  • The earlier SPF is adopted, the better — UV damage is cumulative and begins from childhood. In terms of topical anti-ageing products, introducing antioxidants (vitamin C) and a light retinol in your late 20s to 30s is a sensible preventive approach. But there is benefit to starting at any age.
