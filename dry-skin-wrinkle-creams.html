Wrinkle creams for dry skin — rich moisturisers and what helps most

Dry skin and fine lines often go hand in hand — dehydration makes lines more visible and the skin barrier less resilient. This guide covers the ingredients and product types most useful for dry skin.

Dry skin and dryness lines — the connection

Dry skin lacks sufficient oil (sebum) or moisture, leading to a skin barrier that is less effective at holding water. This results in skin that feels tight, rough, or flaky — and fine lines that appear more pronounced. "Dryness lines" are shallow lines that become visibly worse when skin is dehydrated and soften when skin is well moisturised.

For many women with dry skin, improving hydration dramatically improves the appearance of fine lines — sometimes more noticeably than any active ingredient. Sorting out dryness is often the most effective first step.

Rich moisturisers — what to look for

Dry skin needs moisturisers with a combination of humectants (to draw water in), emollients (to smooth and soften), and occlusives (to seal moisture in). A rich cream or balm texture is usually more suitable than a gel or lightweight fluid for dry skin.

Humectant

Hyaluronic acid

Draws moisture to the skin. Apply to slightly damp skin for best effect, then seal with a richer moisturiser on top. Essential for dry skin routines.

Humectant

Glycerin

One of the most effective and well-tolerated hydrating ingredients available. Found in most good moisturisers. Draws water from deeper skin layers and the environment.

Barrier repair

Ceramides

Repair and strengthen the skin barrier, reducing moisture loss. Particularly important for dry skin where the barrier is often compromised. Look for this high on the ingredients list.

Emollient

Squalane

A lightweight yet rich oil that mimics the skin's natural sebum. Absorbs well without leaving a greasy residue. Excellent for dry skin and compatible with most other ingredients.

Occlusive

Shea butter

A rich emollient and mild occlusive that seals in moisture. Works well in richer night creams. Well tolerated by most skin types including sensitive dry skin.

Occlusive

Petroleum jelly (petrolatum)

The most effective occlusive available. Not a moisturiser on its own, but when applied over damp skin or on top of other products, it seals in moisture extremely effectively. Often used as a final step at night for very dry skin.

Night creams for dry skin

Night creams tend to be richer and more occlusive than daytime moisturisers — and that makes them particularly well suited to dry skin. Look for night creams with ceramides, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, and a rich emollient base. Apply as the last step in your evening routine.

For very dry or compromised skin, a technique known as "slugging" — applying a thin layer of petroleum jelly or a barrier balm as the absolute final step — can significantly support overnight barrier repair. It is not suitable for oily or acne-prone skin, but can work well for dry and mature skin.

Dryness lines — hydration vs. actives

If you notice that your fine lines are particularly pronounced in the morning, worse in winter, or improve noticeably after moisturising — dryness is likely a significant factor. In this case, prioritising hydration (hyaluronic acid serum + rich moisturiser) will often produce more visible improvement than starting retinol or other actives immediately.

Once your hydration foundation is solid, you can introduce retinol or peptides gradually. But hydration first is the right order for dry skin.

Retinol and dry skin — extra care needed

Retinol can cause dryness and irritation, which is compounded on already-dry skin. If you want to use retinol with dry skin:

  • Start at a very low concentration (0.025%)
  • Use just once a week initially
  • Always buffer with a moisturiser — apply moisturiser first, then retinol
  • Follow immediately with a rich moisturiser
  • Ensure your skin's hydration is well managed before introducing retinol at all

A morning routine for dry skin

  1. Gentle cream or micellar cleanser — or water only
  2. Hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin
  3. Rich moisturiser with ceramides and glycerin
  4. SPF 30–50 (look for a formulation that is not drying — many fluid SPFs suit dry skin well)

An evening routine for dry skin

  1. Balm or oil-based cleanser first (if wearing SPF/makeup), then gentle second cleanse
  2. Hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin
  3. Treatment step (retinol once or twice a week — when skin is ready) or peptide serum
  4. Rich night cream with ceramides, shea butter, or squalane
  5. Optional: thin layer of petroleum jelly over very dry areas as a final seal
