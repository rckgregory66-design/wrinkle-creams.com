Creams for fine lines — what works and why

Fine lines are often one of the first signs of changing skin. Understanding what type of line you are dealing with — and what actually helps — makes choosing the right cream much simpler.

Fine lines versus deeper wrinkles

Fine lines are shallow, hairline creases that appear in areas of movement or thin skin — most commonly around the eyes, mouth, and forehead. They are different from deeper-set wrinkles, which are more established folds in the skin often linked to years of sun exposure and collagen loss.

This distinction matters because fine lines are much more responsive to topical skincare than deeper wrinkles are. A well-hydrated skin genuinely does look smoother — fine lines become more visible when skin is dry, and less visible when skin is well moisturised.

Hydration lines

Some fine lines appear or worsen primarily because of dehydration. If you notice that your fine lines are more visible at certain times — first thing in the morning, in cold weather, or when you are unwell — dehydration may be a significant factor. These lines often respond noticeably to a good hydrating routine.

Key hydrating ingredients: hyaluronic acid, glycerin, aloe vera, urea (in low concentrations).

Eye area lines

Fine lines under and around the eyes are among the most common concerns. The skin here is very thin and has fewer oil glands than the rest of the face, making it more prone to dryness and fine lines. See our dedicated eye wrinkle cream guide for more detail.

Forehead lines

Forehead lines are often expression lines — caused by years of raising eyebrows and frowning. Consistent hydration, retinol (with a careful introduction), and daily SPF can help slow further development, but these lines are less responsive to skincare than dehydration-related fine lines. See our forehead wrinkles guide.

Smile lines

Lines at the corners of the mouth (nasolabial folds) deepen with age and expression. Topical skincare can support skin health around them and slow deepening with good SPF and moisture habits, but cannot reverse established folds. These are also influenced by volume loss beneath the skin, which topical creams cannot address.

Ingredients to look for

Hydration

Hyaluronic acid

Attracts water to the skin. Makes fine dehydration lines look visibly smoother. Suitable for all skin types.

Renewal

Retinol

Supports skin cell turnover. Over months of consistent use, may improve fine line appearance and skin texture. Start slowly.

Firmness

Peptides

Support skin structure over time. Generally well tolerated. A gentler option than retinol for those with sensitive skin.

Barrier

Ceramides

Repair and strengthen the skin barrier. Reduce moisture loss. Especially helpful for dry, sensitised, or mature skin.

A simple morning and evening routine for fine lines

Morning

  1. Gentle cleanser

    A low-foaming, fragrance-free cleanser that doesn't strip the skin barrier.

  2. Hyaluronic acid serum (optional)

    Apply to slightly damp skin for best results before moisturiser.

  3. Moisturiser

    A moisturiser suited to your skin type. Look for ceramides, glycerin, or peptides.

  4. SPF 30–50

    The most important step for preventing further fine line development. Apply every morning.

Evening

  1. Cleanse

    Remove the day, SPF, and makeup thoroughly but gently.

  2. Treatment (2–3 nights per week once adapted)

    Retinol, peptide serum, or a targeted treatment. Not every night until your skin is used to it.

  3. Rich moisturiser

    Evenings are a good time for a richer cream. Look for ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and peptides.

  4. Eye cream (optional)

    If the eye area is a concern, a dedicated eye cream can be applied after moisturiser.

Frequently asked questions

  • No single cream cures fine lines, but formulas containing retinol, peptides, hyaluronic acid, and SPF consistently perform best in the evidence. Retinol has the strongest research for actually improving cell turnover over time; hyaluronic acid improves hydration which makes lines look softer temporarily.

  • Fine lines are caused by a combination of natural skin ageing (collagen and elastin decline), UV damage, repeated facial muscle movements, dehydration, and lifestyle factors such as smoking and sun exposure. UV damage is the single biggest external contributor — which is why SPF is the most effective preventative step.

  • Fine lines cannot be fully reversed, but their appearance can be improved. Consistent use of retinol over several months can reduce the depth and visibility of fine lines by encouraging cell turnover and collagen stimulation. Good hydration makes fine lines look softer. Very deep set wrinkles respond better to clinical treatments than to topical products.

  • Fine lines commonly begin appearing in the mid-to-late 20s, most often around the eyes and forehead. They become more noticeable through the 30s and 40s. Sun exposure, smoking, and poor sleep can accelerate their appearance.

  • No. Many mid-range products contain the same evidence-backed ingredients (retinol, peptides, hyaluronic acid) as premium ones. Check the INCI list and look for actives in the top half of the ingredients list rather than paying for packaging and branding.
