Neck wrinkle creams — what helps neck lines and crepey skin

The neck is often one of the first areas where skin ageing becomes visible — and one of the most commonly neglected in skincare routines. Here is a clear guide to caring for the neck and chest area.

Why the neck needs specific attention

The skin on the neck is thinner than facial skin, has fewer oil glands, and is often under-moisturised because people focus their skincare on the face alone. The neck is also exposed to UV radiation daily — usually without SPF protection, which accelerates ageing in this area significantly.

There are also the unique mechanical stresses on neck skin: looking down at phones and screens, sleeping positions, and natural movement over decades all contribute to neck lines and crease patterns.

Neck lines

Horizontal neck lines (often called "necklace lines") are partly structural — related to natural skin anatomy and decades of movement. They become more visible as skin loses elasticity and collagen over time. SPF use and consistent moisturisation can slow their development, but established neck lines are difficult to address topically.

Vertical neck bands are often related to the platysma muscle (the muscle running down the front of the neck) and are less responsive to topical skincare.

Crepey-looking skin

Crepey skin on the neck has a fine, paper-like appearance caused by a combination of thin skin, loss of elasticity, reduced fat, and dehydration. It is worsened by sun damage and dryness. While topical skincare cannot reverse it, consistent moisturisation — particularly with products containing ceramides, glycerin, and hyaluronic acid — and daily SPF can improve the appearance and prevent further progression.

SPF for neck and chest — a commonly missed step

This is the single most impactful change many women can make for neck skin: extend your SPF application below the chin. Apply your morning SPF all the way down the neck and onto the chest and décolletage. Pigmentation and skin ageing in this area is overwhelmingly caused by UV exposure — and most people apply SPF only to the face.

Simple but significant

Extending your daily SPF to your neck and chest is one of the most effective steps you can take for skin in this area. It takes ten extra seconds and makes a genuine long-term difference.

Moisturisers for the neck

Many women use their regular facial moisturiser on the neck, which is perfectly fine. Products with ceramides, hyaluronic acid, glycerin, and peptides are good choices. Rich cream textures tend to suit the neck area, which is often drier than the face. Apply with upward strokes from collar bone to chin.

Some products are marketed specifically as "neck creams" — these often have similar or identical formulations to regular face creams at a higher price. The ingredients matter more than the labelling.

Retinol on the neck — extra sensitivity

Retinol can be extended to the neck area but the skin here can be more sensitive than the face. If you are using retinol on your face and want to extend it to your neck:

  • Start with an even lower concentration or frequency than you use on your face
  • Apply lightly and avoid heavily creasing areas
  • If irritation occurs, reduce frequency or stop
  • Always apply SPF the next morning — even more important when retinol is applied to the exposed neck area

Application tips

  • Apply moisturiser and SPF in upward strokes from the chest to the chin
  • Do not drag the skin downward, which can worsen lines over time
  • Apply skincare to the neck as part of your face routine — it only takes seconds more
  • Consider a richer cream for the neck, particularly in colder months
  • Sleeping on your back reduces sleep compression lines on the neck — though this is difficult to change for most people

Frequently asked questions

  • The neck responds to the same evidence-backed ingredients as the face — retinol, peptides, and hyaluronic acid. A dedicated 'neck cream' is not always necessary; extending your regular face moisturiser and SPF down to your neck and décolletage is a good starting point. Neck-specific products are often a marketing distinction rather than a formulation one.

  • The neck is often neglected in skincare routines — people apply SPF and moisturiser to the face but stop at the jaw. The neck also has thinner skin and fewer sebaceous glands than the face, making it more prone to dryness and UV damage. Downward-looking habits (including screen use) can also accelerate horizontal neck lines.

  • Apply products in upward strokes from the chest to the jaw. This avoids dragging downward on already loose skin. Extend your full routine (cleanser, serum, moisturiser, and SPF) to include the neck and upper chest.

  • Neck lines fall into two types: horizontal lines (sometimes called necklace lines) that are largely genetic and present from a young age, deepening with age; and vertical bands from the platysma muscle, which become more visible as skin loosens. UV damage and dehydration accelerate both. Topical skincare can improve texture and hydration but has limited effect on deep structural changes.

  • Peptides are particularly well-suited to neck skin because they are gentle and support skin firmness. Retinol can also be effective but should be used with care on the neck, which can be more sensitive than the face. Hyaluronic acid and ceramides provide hydration and barrier support. SPF applied daily to the neck is the most important preventive step.
